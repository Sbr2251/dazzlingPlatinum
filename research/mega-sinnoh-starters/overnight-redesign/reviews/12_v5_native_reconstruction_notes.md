# v5 Native Reconstruction Notes

## Infernape front landmark study

Platinum’s canonical Infernape succeeds through a compact exposed face, a bright chest plane, clearly separated bent limbs, large feet, and a flame mass that follows rather than hides the head. The rejected v4 Mega instead places a large nearly white triangular mane over most of the head and upper torso, uses a narrow dark trunk for the body, and routes the staff through the same value range as the arms and outline. The result is transformed but anatomically unclear.

For v5, retain the Monkey King transformation but rebuild it from native landmarks: a smaller swept pale mane behind an exposed warm face; a broad red-brown chest/waist; two readable bent legs and larger feet; one gold-brown diagonal staff with a light center and dark edge; two explicit hand clusters overlapping the shaft; and compact endpoint flames. The staff, arms, and torso must occupy distinct palette ramps. Frame two may move the mane tips and endpoint flames but must not redraw or detach either grip.

## Torterra front landmark study

Canonical Torterra remains readable because the white shell rim sweeps cleanly behind a large face, the jaw and eye are isolated, each leg has a distinct foot plane, and the tree sits behind the body rather than replacing it. v4 keeps the right broad concept but compresses the body into a low, dark horizontal strip. Its pale fortress dome dominates the head, its four legs become repeated thin columns, and the canopy forms a heavy flat roof.

For v5, enlarge and move the head forward; give it a single bright eye pixel, a cream jaw, a dark nostril, and a strong horn/cheek outline. Build two visible near legs as broad stepped masses with separate claws and use the far legs only as supporting silhouettes. Replace the pale slab with a green/stone armored shell dome whose rim connects visibly behind the shoulder. Reduce the tree canopy by roughly one quarter and lift it enough to expose the shell. Use large brown underside clusters rather than vertical noise. Frame two should alter canopy tips, head angle, and one foreleg by a pixel while preserving the shell/body connection.

## Empoleon front landmark study

Canonical Empoleon reads instantly because its head and gold trident are one vertical unit, the white breast wedge defines the torso, each flipper grows from a visible shoulder, and both feet sit below the body. v4 has strong emperor mass and longer wing-blades, but its head is buried in a broad dark block, the gold crown floats over an indistinct face, the shoulders merge with the mantle, and tiny isolated feet fail to support the body.

For v5, rebuild an exposed navy head and beak beneath a shorter, thicker crown; taper a bright breastplate from the neck to a broad lower torso; show shoulder root clusters before each blade-wing; keep the wing span wide but shorten the outer tips; add larger gold feet that overlap the body base; and reserve the darkest navy for the outline and inner mantle folds. The crown must join the head in every frame. Frame two can raise wing tips and alter mantle highlights, but shoulder roots and feet remain connected.

## Infernape rear landmark study

Canonical rear Infernape exposes a broad back, one strong shoulder and forearm plane, bent rear legs, and a head flame that arcs behind the skull. v4 is technically rear-facing but the pale mane again hides the neck and shoulders; the torso is a narrow dark diamond; the legs are thin; and the staff visually crosses the figure without two unmistakable rear-side hand contacts.

For v5 rear, show a broad red-brown shoulder/back mass below a smaller mane, keep both legs thick and asymmetrical, expose a short tail or rear cloth/flame cluster without inventing a new major feature, and place the same diagonal staff behind one forearm but in front of both explicit hands. Staff endpoint flames must remain compact and attached. The rear palette should prioritize dark back planes with warm rim light, not a white mane slab.

## Torterra rear landmark study

Canonical rear Torterra clearly layers canopy, trunk, shell spikes, domed shell, hindquarter, and overlapping legs. v4 preserves a fortress concept, but the circular pale shell rim and four near-identical brown supports make it read as a table or stump. The dark animal body is almost absent, the shell lacks armor planes, and the rear legs do not establish weight or perspective.

For v5 rear, replace the full circular rim with an asymmetric dark shell dome and three stepped stone battlements; expose a broad dark-green hindquarter below it; paint one large near rear leg and one smaller far leg with clear joints; let only two structural shell roots descend into the body; anchor the trunk into the shell with a visible earth mound; and retain the canopy as a secondary mass rather than the whole identity. The head may be minimally visible at one side, but the view must remain unmistakably rear-facing.

## Empoleon rear landmark study

Canonical rear Empoleon is simple but unambiguous: the head and trident rise from a single dark back mass, two blue shoulder ridges define orientation, the wings open from the sides, and small gold back markings sit on the torso. v4 rear still resembles a mirrored front because the bright white breast/mantle shapes dominate the center. Its crown is fragmented, blade wings are only thin edge ornaments, and the feet are isolated lines.

For v5 rear, eliminate the central white breast wedge entirely. Build one broad navy back with cobalt shoulder armor, a short integrated gold crown, and two blade wings rooted behind the shoulders. Use only small pale mantle-edge highlights on the sides, add gold back insignia instead of a breastplate, and paint two larger feet under the lower body. One wing may angle differently for depth, but both must remain attached in both frames.
